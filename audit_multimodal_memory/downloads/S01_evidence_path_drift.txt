5319: Management evolution can therefore change not only performance, but also which
5320: representations continue to support that performance.
5321: This creates a stability problem that task accuracy alone may not reveal. A
5322: revised policy can maintain or even improve short-term answers while relying less
5323: on the modality that originally grounded them. For example, a system may answer
5324: correctly from a cached textual summary while rarely reopening the visual
5325: evidence from which that summary was formed. If the summary later becomes wrong,
5326: the loss of that evidence path may only become visible much later. Current
5327: policy-evolution methods rarely evaluate whether adaptation changes the balance
5328: of evidence used across representations.
5329: A useful direction is to evaluate policy changes together with the evidence
5330: paths they alter. Before a new policy is accepted, the system should check not
5331: only whether downstream performance improves, but also whether access to
5332: supporting modalities and representations has been weakened. This may require
5333: tracking which memory paths support important decisions and comparing that use
5334: before and after adaptation. What remains open is how much change in evidence
5335: use is healthy adaptation and when it becomes a loss of grounding that should
5336: block or reverse the policy update.
