391: Beyond providing a unified taxonomy, this study offers three broader contributions. First, we provide comprehensive coverage of multimodal agent memory across modalities, tasks, architectures, and evaluation settings, including a corpus of 297 memory architectures and 82 benchmark records in the current version of our taxonomy. Second, by jointly analyzing memory representation and management, we identify cross-cutting patterns that are difficult to observe from either dimension alone, including the prevalence of dynamic memory updates, the widespread use of hybrid representations, and the relative scarcity of deployment-time memory-policy evolution. Third, we synthesize the limitations of current systems and derive future directions around recoverable and provenance-aware memory, temporal validity and cross-representation consistency, self-improving memory management, bounded lifelong memory, governed forgetting, and lifecycle-aware evaluation.
