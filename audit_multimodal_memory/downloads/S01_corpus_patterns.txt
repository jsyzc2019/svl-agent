2633: show that multimodal agent memory architectures concentrate in T2 Dynamic Memory across modalities and in the overall corpus, while T3 Self-Evolving Memory remains relatively rare. Of the 297 architectures, 221 (74.4%) update memory state or its management policy during operation. T2 Dynamic Memory accounts for 204 architectures (68.7% of the corpus) that update memory through stable management policies, whereas T3 Self-Evolving Memory accounts for 17 architectures (5.7%) that update their management policies based on deployment-time feedback. Stable management policies allow agents to incorporate new experience through predictable and controllable update procedures, making them the dominant approach to memory updating.
2634: Hybrid representations help address the competing requirements of multimodal memory. Hybrid systems constitute 139 of the 297 architectures (46.8%), making them the largest representation category. Their prevalence suggests that a single representation type is often insufficient to balance representational fidelity, preservation of modality-specific detail, retrieval efficiency, and storage cost for rich multimodal experiences. Hybrid architectures are therefore the most common representational choice, but they do not dominate the landscape: many systems address narrower memory problems using the single representation type best suited to their tasks.
2635: Compact and explicit memory artifacts are the predominant target for deployment-time policy evolution. Of the 17 T3 Self-Evolving Memory architectures, 13 are cross-represented systems, while the other 4 are hybrid systems that include an explicit representation. By contrast, to the best of our knowledge, neither the source-preserving category nor the latent or parametric category contains a T3 Self-Evolving Memory system under our definition. Explicit rules, skills, workflows, retrieval descriptions, and programs occupy a useful middle ground for deployment-time evolution: they are more compact and directly editable than detailed source observations, while remaining more inspectable and controllable than information distributed across latent states or model parameters. These properties provide identifiable targets that can be revised from feedback, validated, and reused in subsequent tasks.
2636: The analysis above characterizes multimodal agent memory at the logical level: how experience is represented, how memory state and management policies may change, and what requirements arise from their interaction.
