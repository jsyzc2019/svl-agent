## 一手逐字核验（Europe PMC 原文摘要）

**§4ek**：
> "past work either has not actually measured probabilistic predictions but rather point predictions or used sequences that were not independent… high-powered, preregistered studies… 750 adult Amazon Mechanical Turk workers… probabilistic predictions for truly independent sequences. In contrast to point predictions, which generated a significant gambler's fallacy, probabilistic predictions were not found to lead to a gambler's fallacy… the point predictions could not be reconstructed by sampling from the probability judgments. This suggests that the gambler's fallacy originates at the decision stage rather than in probabilistic reasoning… New theories… may be needed."

**§4el**：
> "The gambler's fallacy is the tendency to expect random processes to switch more often than they actually do… It's often taken to be evidence for irrationality. It isn't. Rather, it is to be expected from a group of Bayesians who begin with causal uncertainty, and then observe unbiased data from an (in fact) statistically independent process… they do so in an asymmetric way – ruling out 'streaky' hypotheses more quickly than 'switchy' ones… the majority (and the average) exhibit the gambler's fallacy… If they have limited memory, this tendency persists even with arbitrarily-large amounts of data… what's been thought to be evidence for irrationality may instead be rational responses to limited data and memory."
